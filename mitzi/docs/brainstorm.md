# Brainstorm log — how the idea was chosen

2026-11-24. The starting question: what could AR glasses do that would help something in the world? Uses already seen elsewhere: medicine, teleprompters, golf, games, casual consumer use.

## The filter

Most AR glasses ideas are a phone app worn on your face. An idea earns the glasses only if at least one of these is true:

1. **Your hands are busy.**
2. **Your eyes have to stay on the world.**
3. **The face itself is the sensor** (glasses see what you see, hear what you hear, and sit next to your eyes).

A second lesson: the display is the hardest and most expensive part. Some useful smart glasses need no display at all.

## Every idea considered

### Round 1 — health
| Idea | What it does | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Gait-freeze breaker | Draws cue lines on the floor for people with Parkinson's whose steps freeze | Strong health story; needs clinical supervision. Parked |
| Blind-spot remapper | Moves what's hidden by a blind spot into working vision | Needs heavy video processing and better optics. Parked |
| Memory aid for early dementia | Quietly names people and things | Big payoff; serious privacy and consent questions. Parked |
| Fatigue and concussion check | Tracks blinks and eye steadiness; no display | Needs an eye-facing camera. Parked |
| Remote expert | A specialist far away sees what a health worker sees | Easiest to build on existing kits. Parked |

### Round 2 — other angles
| Idea | What it does | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Live subtitles | Translated captions of real speech in the glasses | Reuses the Live Interpreter pipeline; caption glasses already exist. Parked |
| Stroke "neglect" coach | Prompts the wearer to look toward the side they ignore | Original; testers hard to find. Parked |
| Stutter-easing audio | Plays the wearer's voice back with a tiny delay; no display | Cheap; in-ear devices already exist. Parked |
| Pill check | Names the pill in your hand | A phone does most of it. Dropped |
| Glasses on the helper | Put the glasses on the caregiver, not the patient | A design angle worth reusing |

### Round 3 — much wider
| Idea | What it does | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Air glasses | Shows air pollution as you walk | Parked |
| Leak finder | Gas and heat-loss map for workers and landlords | Parked |
| Shock warning | Warns an electrician near a live wire | Parked |

## Why language immersion won
- **Personal.** A real person to learn for, and a first learner starting from zero.
- **It passes the filter.** Learning happens while cooking and cleaning, with eyes and hands on the world.
- **It's buildable.** The learning can be proven on a phone first; the display only has to show a few characters.
- **Research backs the base idea,** and the combination we want doesn't exist yet ([research](research.md)).
- **A memory behind it:** a family friend's house where every object had a sticker with its Spanish name.

## How the idea grew in the same session
1. Labels in English and Japanese →
2. labels that fade as each word is learned →
3. an Anki-style deck that builds itself from your own objects →
4. a simulated conversation with a "person" (Suki) →
5. the conversation partner becomes the centre, because nouns alone don't make conversation →
6. Obaachan mode for rehearsing talk with an elder →
7. topic packs by interest and industry.
